# Target Group Analysis — Privacy-First AI for Solo Professionals

**Context:** Pivot from dev-focused AI infra to privacy-first AI for non-technical professionals. 1-person company needs solo decision-makers who buy fast.

## Filter Criteria (all 6 must pass)

1.  **Solo decision-maker** — no procurement committee, your 1-person company can close them
2.  **High income** — $200+/hr billing rate
3.  **Real privacy need** — regulatory or reputational risk, not just preference
4.  **High-value unstructured data** — client notes, strategies, records worth AI-searching
5.  **Accessible** — reachable via content, communities, or warm networks
6.  **Pain is current** — already feeling it, not a future problem
7.  **Amplification potential** — principal is public-facing (YouTube, podcasts, stages, LinkedIn) and will organically spread the word

## CRITICAL: Small Teams, Not Solo Users

Wealth/financial coaches typically run **teams of ~10 people** (associates, assistants, analysts, admins). This means:

*   **Multi-user from day 1** — shared workspace, not single-user vault
*   **Role-based access** — principal sees everything, associate sees only their clients, admin sees scheduling only
*   **Team collaboration** — "summarize what the team discussed about client X" is a real query
*   **Knowledge handoff** — when an associate leaves, their client context stays in the system
*   **Billing is per-workspace, not per-user** — the principal pays, the team uses

**Implication:** The product is closer to Notion-for-teams-with-AI than it is to a personal tool. The principal is the buyer, but 10 people are the users. Onboarding the team is part of the sale.

## CRITICAL: Amplification Potential

The principal's public presence is a distribution channel. Groups where the principal **IS the brand** will organically spread the product — on stages, in podcasts, in newsletters, on social — because using cutting-edge AI tools **enhances their own brand**. This is free, credible, and compounds.

## Groups That Pass All Criteria (sorted by amplification potential)

### #1 Executive / Business Coaches — Amplification: VERY HIGH

*   $300-1000/hr principal, small team (2-5 people)
*   **Privacy trigger:** Board-level discussions, strategic plans, personal assessments
*   **Data:** Coaching session notes, 360 feedback, development plans
*   **Entry point:** ICF network, LinkedIn, podcast appearances
*   **Why amplification is highest:** They live on LinkedIn, run podcasts, speak at conferences, write books. Their audience IS their funnel. Being seen as tech-forward enhances their brand. They will mention your tool on stage, in podcasts, in newsletters — unprompted, because it makes them look good. Their audience (other professionals) overlaps with your next customer segments.

### #2 Wealth / Financial Coaches — Amplification: HIGH (subset-dependent)

*   $200-500/hr principal, ~10-person team
*   **Privacy trigger:** Client financial data, net worth, tax strategies — NDAs standard
*   **Data:** Client session notes, financial plans, goal tracking, team meeting summaries, email threads
*   **Entry point:** LinkedIn content, coaching communities
*   **Team dynamics:** Principal coaches HNW clients, associates prep research, admins manage scheduling and docs. Knowledge is scattered across the team — that's the pain.
*   **Amplification caveat:** Mixed bag. YouTube finance creators (big reach) are very public. But many HNW wealth coaches operate quietly — old money doesn't Instagram. Filter for the public-facing subset. The loud ones are your amplifiers.

### #3 Celebrity / Elite Fitness Coaches — Amplification: HIGH (reach, wrong audience)

*   $150-400/session, client rosters are their IP, small team (2-5)
*   **Privacy trigger:** Celebrity health data, training programs, nutrition plans — paparazzi/leak risk
*   **Data:** Client profiles, program design notes, progress tracking
*   **Entry point:** Instagram, niche fitness communities
*   **Amplification caveat:** Massive raw reach (100K-1M followers on Instagram is normal). One story = your entire month's marketing. BUT their audience is fitness fans, not your next customer segment. Good for general awareness, weak for qualified leads. Secondary amplifier, not primary.

### #4 Management Consultants (Solo/Small) — Amplification: MEDIUM

*   $200-600/hr, client-sensitive work, 2-5 person teams
*   **Privacy trigger:** Client NDA, competitive intelligence, M&A data
*   **Data:** Engagement notes, analysis docs, client correspondence
*   **Entry point:** LinkedIn, consulting communities
*   **Amplification caveat:** Active on LinkedIn and Substack, speak at events. But their audience is other consultants — narrow funnel for your product. Good for credibility within the niche, weak for cross-vertical expansion.

### #5 Family Office Advisors — Amplification: LOW

*   Retainer-based, ultra-HNW families, ~5-10 person team
*   **Privacy trigger:** Family financial, legal, personal data all in one place
*   **Data:** Investment notes, family meeting summaries, estate planning docs
*   **Entry point:** Family office networks, wealth conferences
*   **Amplification caveat:** By definition, invisible. Their whole job is discretion. They will never mention your product publicly. High-value customers but zero organic distribution.

### #6 Therapists / Psychologists — Amplification: VERY LOW

*   $120-250/hr, may have 2-3 associate therapists + admin
*   **Privacy trigger:** Therapy confidentiality, ethical obligation
*   **Data:** Session notes, treatment plans, client correspondence
*   **Entry point:** Psychology Today, professional associations
*   **Amplification caveat:** Ethical obligation to maintain confidentiality extends to themselves. They don't public-speak about their tools. Wrong fit for amplification, though viable as expansion vertical later.

### #7 Luxury Real Estate Agents — Amplification: MEDIUM (wrong audience)

*   High commission per deal, VIP clients, small team (3-5)
*   **Privacy trigger:** Client financials, preferences, negotiations — competitive intel
*   **Data:** Client dossiers, property notes, deal histories
*   **Entry point:** Realtor networks, LinkedIn
*   **Amplification caveat:** Public-facing on social media, but their followers are home buyers — not your target customer. Noise without signal.

## Groups That Sound Good But FAIL

*   **Lawyers** — Risk-averse, slow adopters, want SOC 2/certifications a 1-person company can't provide. Come back later.
*   **Doctors** — HIPAA is brutal, liability exposure massive, insurance requirements kill it for solo startup.
*   **Accountants** — Regulatory gatekeepers, slow procurement, risk-averse.
*   **Insurance companies** — Closed system, no 1-person vendor.

## Recommended Beachhead Strategy

**Primary target (for customers + amplification):** Executive/business coaches

*   Highest amplification potential — they ARE the distribution channel
*   Audience overlap with other target segments (their followers = your next customers)
*   Using AI makes them look cutting-edge = organic mentions unprompted

**Secondary target (for revenue + team scale):** Wealth/financial coaches (public-facing subset)

*   Larger teams (~10) = higher per-workspace revenue
*   Filter for YouTube/podcast-active coaches only
*   Same product, different vertical messaging

**Expansion path:** Executive coaches → wealth coaches → management consultants → therapists → family offices → (later, with compliance certs) lawyers, doctors
